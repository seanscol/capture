# Operating notes — constraints from the archive

**Version 2.8 · 2026-09-06** — versioned as a set with `SYSTEM.md`, because
these files ship together and the drift machinery keys on this line. The
number tracks the release, not this file's content, which changes rarely.

**Status:** derived from a 2023–2026 personal archive (137,000 words, eight
documents) by a chat with full read access. Every item is a regularity
observable across four years of writing. Sean has read the source analysis
and agrees with it. `[SEAN 2026-09-04]`

**This file is deliberately content-free.** The source analysis quoted
biography — bereavement, illness, relationships, named people — as evidence.
None of that is here, at Sean's instruction, and the reason is a design
argument rather than privacy alone: **the lessons are structural, and
intimate detail sitting in a spec is a standing invitation for a session to
mistake salience for relevance and build a rule out of it.** Line citations
are kept so the source can be found; their descriptions are not.

**Nothing here is clinical.** These are patterns in a corpus, not claims
about a person.

**Why this file exists:** a planning session working from a task list will
build the plan it is asked for, agree the priorities it is given, and treat
volume as importance. Each item below is a case where that default is wrong.

---

## 0. The two that outrank the rest

Sean has named these himself as the ones that matter. `[SEAN 2026-09-04]`

**He remembers the architecture of a life better than living it** (item 9).
**Nothing gets completed** (item 3). Everything else below is downstream of
these two, and a session that honours the other nine while ignoring these
has helped with the wrong thing.

**The operational form, which binds this project and not just its user:**
designing the system is the pattern running. The spec may grow only in a
version where something shipped; it may shrink at any time. When the choice
is between recording something and building something, build.

## 1. A new plan is usually a symptom

More than twenty distinct full daily routines across four years
(`2023` L1955, L1972, L1989, L1997, L2185, L2210, L2221, L2300, L2318,
L2336, L2367, L2546, L2570, L2599, L2614; `2025` L699, L734, L1296;
`Early_2026`; `Work_Hobbies` p.1). They differ in detail and are identical
in shape. No evidence any ran for a month.

**Operational:** when asked for a new routine, system, schedule or
framework, retrieve the previous one first and ask what happened to it.
Build it if he still wants it — but with the previous version on screen
while he decides.

## 2. Conclusions get re-derived because they cannot be found

The archive contains two near-identical rule lists, the second headed
*"Updated rules - ignore previous set."* The first had been lost. Three
further instances of a conclusion reached, recorded once, and never
retrieved (`2024` L1680; `2023` L4684; `2025` L4481, three years old and
still unbuilt).

**Operational: retrieval before generation.** Before answering "what should
I do about X", check whether he has already answered it. This is the
highest-value function the system can perform and the one the corpus proves
he cannot do unaided. **If he asks something already answered, assume he
cannot find it — never that he is re-litigating.**

## 3. Nothing closes, because completion is never recorded

In 137,000 words there is no record of anything being finished — no done
column, no dates, no "shipped." One item recurs across all seven documents
from 2023 to now, still phrased as a thing to start.

**Operational:** any app holding a list needs a completion channel *and* a
decay mechanism — items periodically re-asked, "is this still true?"
Without both, lists grow monotonically forever, which is what the archive
shows happening.

## 4. Repetition is not priority; the asterisks are

The most-written-about items are the least finished. He already runs a
hand-applied one-to-seven asterisk notation.

**Operational:** asterisks are the priority signal, frequency is the anxiety
signal. Any ranking that reads repetition or recency as importance inverts
his actual priorities.

## 5. Plan scope inflates as capacity drops

The most expansive plan in the archive — roughly thirty deliverables over
eighteen months, nine hires, a large fundraise (`2025` L2526–2610, L4654) —
comes from the period of lowest capacity. The same long-range projection is
typed out twice, unchanged, hundreds of lines apart (`2025` L6177, L6688).

**Operational:** track plan size as a number and show it back. *"This week's
plan is four times last month's"* is a fact statable without interpretation,
and it describes what he wrote rather than predicting what he will do, so it
does not breach §2.6. **This is his own idea** — the archive already
specifies an instrument for exactly this. It just doesn't exist yet.

## 6. He does not defer, and should not be handled by deferring

`2025` L674–695. He is not against expert advice; he is unwilling to wait
on it.

**Operational:** give evidence with its quality attached, and the question
he could put to someone qualified. Advice framed as "wait and see" will be
discarded and will cost trust in whatever it arrived with.

## 7. Long prose resolves; lists never do

The three passages in four years that resolve anything (`2023` L3934;
`2024` L4793; `2024` L1680) are long, written in one sitting, and
unbulleted. Every list is forward-facing and closes nothing.

**Operational:** when something is unresolved, the useful prompt is "write
it out long", not "break it into tasks." **Breaking an unresolved thing into
subtasks is the failure mode the archive demonstrates most consistently.**
This lands directly on the capture service — see §10.

## 8. Structural remedies applied to non-structural problems

A recurring class of problem gets twenty variations of the same list-shaped
answer across four years, and the outcome never arrives. `2025` L4481
contains the corpus's own diagnosis of why, and is still unbuilt.

**Operational:** when a list is proposed for the twentieth time against an
unchanged outcome, that is a falsified strategy being repeated, not a plan
needing refinement.

## 9. Bias every surface toward the past tense

Thousands of lines about what will be done; almost none about what was
done (`2024` L311).

**Operational:** *"what happened today"* is the scarce input. He does not
need help generating future. This cuts against the grain of a task app and
should be resolved deliberately rather than by default.

## 10. Register: neither hype nor sympathy

Overstatement and self-criticism appear in adjacent sentences. Mirroring
either amplifies it.

**Operational:** flat, specific, numerate. Show the data. §4 already says
"show him numbers, not reassurance"; it generalises from output to tone.

## 11. There is no second person, and the system cannot be one

The archive reaches this independently of §12 D3/Q2, and arrives at a
question mark rather than an answer.

**Operational, and the most important limit here:** the system can surface a
signal early and make it visible. **It cannot be the person who notices.**
Designing as though it can replaces a gap with the appearance of one being
filled, which is worse than the gap. **The right output of this pattern is a
task with a name on it: who gets called.** That task exists on no list.

## The meta-risk

An integrated app system is structurally the twenty-first routine: a
comprehensive framework designed in a period of constrained capacity, more
elaborate than its twenty predecessors, none of which ran for a month.

What would make it different is narrow and specific. **Every previous system
recorded what he intended. This one has to record what happened.** That is
the test to apply to any proposed feature, and it is a sharper form of §9's
*"it must replace checking, not add to it."*

The reason to build it anyway is item 2 — and the way to find out whether
it is the twenty-first routine or not is to have it running and used, not to
specify it further. **A system in use produces evidence. A system being
designed produces documents.**
